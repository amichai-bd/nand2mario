DE10-Lite · electrical setup

A keyboard needs power, pull-ups and safe signal levels.

PS/2 Clock and Data idle high. The keyboard pulls them low to send bits. Use pull-ups, not pull-downs.

With resistors today

Try a 3.3 V supply only if the keyboard works at that voltage. Measure before connecting FPGA signals.

Preferred 5 V solution

Power the keyboard at 5 V and translate both signals with an open-drain-compatible bidirectional shifter.

This is a wiring guide, not a tested keyboard or an implemented receiver. PS/2 remains later input work in the project charter.

Electrical background: Digilent PS/2 description. Its Xilinx-specific resistor circuit is not a DE10-Lite design.

Option A · staged bench procedure

Measure before connecting signal pins.

  1. Power off. Wire common ground, keyboard 3.3 V and the pull-ups. Leave FPGA Clock/Data disconnected. Use continuity mode only unpowered.
  2. Power on; select DC volts. Black probe on ground. Check keyboard VCC, Clock and Data: each should be near 3.3 V while idle.
  3. Stop for a low line or sagging supply. Check wiring and keyboard requirements. An idle-high reading alone does not prove that the keyboard works.
  4. Verify the input-only FPGA configuration. Power off, connect the signals, then power on and test raw received bytes or clock edges.

A multimeter verifies DC levels, not frames or rise time. Do not move keyboard VCC to 5 V while this signal wiring remains attached. Physical acceptance remains separate: bring-up gap.

Option B · conceptual only · receive-only

A 5 V divider needs more than a ratio.

The keyboard pull-up participates in the divider. Account for supply and resistor tolerances, input thresholds, cable capacitance and rise time on both Clock and Data.

No ready-to-wire values are specified for an unknown keyboard. This network is not a bidirectional command interface. A multimeter alone cannot qualify its timing. PS/2 electrical background

Option C · recommended when the keyboard needs 5 V

Translate both open-drain signals.

Choose a BSS138-style open-drain-compatible module; check its pull-ups before adding any. HV/LV are supply references, not a power converter. Verify a suitable 5 V power source and current budget.

After the electrical checks

First receive bytes. Then decode keys.

Start 0 → 8 data bits, LSB first → odd parity → Stop 1

Synchronize Clock and Data into the FPGA clock domain. Filter glitches, detect falling Clock edges, and check framing, odd parity and incomplete-frame timeout.

Expose a clock-edge count or raw bytes first. Releases and extended keys use multi-byte sequences; one byte is not always one key event.

Idle voltage checks do not prove protocol reception. Use a receiver or suitable scope/logic analyzer for that step. This deck adds no RTL and does not claim the current image has a working PS/2 receiver.
